Deception+ v2

Measures how much a pitcher's arm slot, release point, extension, and delivery consistency predict outcomes — independent of pitch velocity, movement, and spin. This is the "mechanics as deception" question: does the way a pitcher's body moves, rather than what the pitch does, make hitters worse off? The model uses only measurable body and release data from Statcast; it cannot see pitch type, velocity, or anything that describes the pitch itself, so it can't re-derive Stuff+ under a different name. Scores are cross-fitted: every pitcher's score comes from models that never trained on their own pitches, so there is no data leakage.

100 = league average for that season. Each 10 points is one standard deviation above or below league average — a score of 120 means a pitcher's mechanics graded roughly 2 SD more deceptive than the league that year. Scores are standardized within each season, so the same score in different years means the same relative standing. Minimum 300 pitches to qualify; below-threshold pitchers are shrunk toward average and marked unqualified.

Version history
  • v1 — proof-of-concept single-season model. Per-request scoring. Split-half r=0.998 was artifact (consistency features computed on full season before splitting). Body features (height, weight) could memorize pitcher identity.
  • v2 — cross-fitted ensemble (pitcher-grouped folds). Removed body identity features (height, weight, arm_angle_std, release_extension_std, release_consistency) per pre-registered identifiability rules. No per-request scoring — all scores pre-computed.

Cross-model overview

Three models, three different questions: Stuff+ asks whether a pitch's raw velocity/movement/spin predicts good outcomes; Deception+ asks whether a pitcher's BODY and release consistency predict good outcomes independent of pitch quality; Tunneling+ asks whether a pitcher's pitches look alike out of the hand and separate late. If they're measuring genuinely different things, they should correlate weakly with each other. Here's whether they do.

Stuff+ and Deception+ share a real, moderate correlation (r≈0.54) — plausibly because pitchers with better raw stuff also tend to have more consistent, repeatable deliveries, not because the models are redundant restatements of each other. Both correlate weakly with Tunneling+ (r≈0.12 and 0.19) — tunnel/plate-separation geometry is capturing something the other two genuinely don't.

Average Stuff+ by pitch type, 2025

The common arsenal pitch types cluster tightly around 96-102 — expected, since the plus-scale is standardized to a ~100 league average overall, not per pitch type. The rare codes left out of this chart (each thrown by fewer than 10 pitchers leaguewide): SC (67), FO (100) — mostly position players in mop-up innings. Knuckleball (KN, 72) is the one genuine, known limitation: real knuckleballers throw slow with little conventional movement by design, so a physically-based model correctly reads their raw characteristics as "bad stuff" while missing the entire reason the pitch works — unpredictability.

Arm angle's real effect on Deception+, decomposed (2025)

● arm_angle alone● release_pos_z alone● combined (real total)

Correction from the first pass of this report: showing arm_angle's SHAP contribution alone was misleading. arm_angle and release_pos_z correlate at r=0.79 (a lower arm slot mechanically means a lower release point), so the tree-based model splits the "unusual delivery" credit between the two nearly-redundant features - arm_angle alone looked like it PENALIZED extreme submarine deliveries, when the model is actually rewarding them heavily, just filing most of that credit under release_pos_z instead. The COMBINED curve is the real, fair picture: true submarine (-62°) is worth +14.1 points combined - a huge reward, confirming the original intuition (Tyler Rogers-style deliveries genuinely are treated as elite deception). Pure over-the-top (67°) goes the OTHER way, -2.8 points - so it's not simply "extreme is rewarded," it's specifically low/submarine slots that are, asymmetrically. Real evidence: Tyler Rogers's own 2025 breakdown shows arm_angle at -3.5 pts but release_pos_z at +17.7 pts, net positive and part of a 133.5 overall Deception+ (one of the best scores in the league that season) - exactly what the combined curve predicts.

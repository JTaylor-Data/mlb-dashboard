1. What It Measures

Location+ grades where a pitch ended up and how valuable that location was, given the count. It fills the deliberate gap left by Stuff+, Deception+, and Tunneling+, all of which exclude plate location by design.

Follows FanGraphs' public Location+ methodology (their Pitching primer) with one deliberate deviation: stand is included (FanGraphs excludes it). The deviation is empirically tested — see §6. p_throws remains excluded, per FanGraphs' reasoning that pitcher handedness alone is a matchup concept, not a location property.

The accepted limitation: Statcast captures where a pitch actually crossed, not catcher target or pitcher intent. This measures location-value-achieved, not intent. FanGraphs tested intent-based data and found it added no predictive value — accepted here for the same reason.

100 = league average. 10 points = 1 SD. Spread is compressed vs. Stuff+ (smaller underlying lever).

4. Scoring Pipeline

Empirical-Bayes shrinkage. K values (pitches needed to trust a sample) are dramatically larger than Stuff+'s, estimated fresh from Location+'s own data. K ranges from ~11 (CS) to ~165 (FF). Reusing Stuff+'s K ≈ 2 here would under-shrink badly — a pitcher's 30-fastball sample would be treated as nearly fully reliable when Location+'s own data says FF needs ~165 pitches.

7. ⚠ Critical Issue — Sign Flip

Location+ v1's ERA and xFIP correlations are negative — a higher score predicts a worse pitcher. This was found in the 2026-09-21 validation study. The v2 candidate corrects this.

8. ⚠ Critical Issue — Stability Collapse After 2023

Year-over-year Pearson r collapsed dramatically in recent seasons. The model was reasonably stable through 2022, then fell apart. Score std = 3.07 (severely compressed, vs. v2's std = 10.0).
Season pairPearson rsklearn R²
2015→2016 through 2021→20220.36–0.52Mixed (−0.09 to +0.20)
2022→20230.811+0.572
2023→20240.090−1.480
2024→20250.009−0.464
Pooled (all pairs)0.361−0.160

The pooled sklearn R² = −0.160 (reported previously as "the YoY number") and pooled Pearson r = 0.361 are both correct — they measure different things. The R² is negative because the score compression (std = 3.07) means "predict everyone at 94" beats using the actual score. The v2 candidate (std ≈ 10) addresses this.

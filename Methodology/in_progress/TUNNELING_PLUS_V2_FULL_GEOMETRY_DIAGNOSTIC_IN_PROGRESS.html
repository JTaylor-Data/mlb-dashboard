Background & Methodology

Tunneling+ v2 uses a decomposed 5-classifier architecture: xSwing%, xCS%, xWhiff%, xFoul%, BIP type×EV → fixed run-value table → per-pitch expected run value → pitcher-season plus scale. Each classifier is trained on the same lag feature set: 6 lag slots × 4 features each = 24 total lag features, comprising:

  • 18 numeric geometry features: lagN_{commit_distance, plate_distance, shape_break} for N=1–6
  • 6 categorical sequence features: lagN_pitch_type for N=1–6

This diagnostic tests whether the 18 numeric geometry features carry independent signal after controlling for pitch-type information. The prior Part 4 test produced z = 180.1 but was near-circular: it bucketed pitches by the model's own full blended xRV output, which already incorporates the geometry features being tested.

Corrected Methodology

For each classifier, we compute SHAP values via shap.TreeExplainer on a 200K stratified sample (30K for BIP due to 20-class multiclass cost). Three SHAP scores are isolated:

  • lag1-geom: sum of SHAP for lag1_{commit_distance, plate_distance, shape_break} (3 features)
  • alllag-geom: sum of SHAP for all 18 geometry features (6 lags × 3 metrics)
  • pt-lag: sum of SHAP for lag1..lag6_pitch_type (6 categorical features)

Each score is tercile-split; a two-proportion z-test compares the high-tercile vs low-tercile real outcome rates. Positive z = high SHAP → more of the outcome = correct direction. Sign inversion = the model uses the feature in the wrong direction.

The xWhiff% result was established first (Part A). The four remaining classifiers are from Part A2. BIP uses an xRV-weighted sum of SHAP values across 20 classes, tested against real delta_run_exp on BIP events.

Interpretation by Classifier

xSwing% — Geometry is Real

Pitches where the model assigns high geometry SHAP are swung at 71.9% of the time vs 33.3% in the low tercile (alllag z = +153.0). This makes physical sense: tunneling geometry — how closely the prior pitch's trajectory matched the current pitch's early path — should influence whether a hitter swings. The model is correctly using geometry to predict swing decisions.

xCS% — Geometry is Real

Similar story: high-geometry tercile produces 15.9% called-strike rate vs 6.1% low (alllag z = +58.4). Pitches that geometrically resemble a prior strike are more likely to be taken for strikes. Correct direction, strong signal.

xWhiff% — Geometry is Null

The corrected test shows lag-1 geometry z = −1.706 (effectively zero). The prior z = 180.1 was near-circular. xWhiff% is learning pitch-type sequencing patterns, not geometric tunneling. High pt-lag z = +18.6 confirms pitch-type sequences are the actual driver of whiff prediction.

xFoul% — Wrong Direction

High geometry SHAP predicts fewer fouls (alllag z = −44.6). This is unexpected and suggests the model has learned a spurious inverse relationship. The pt-lag z = −2.1 is also borderline negative, suggesting the entire lag structure may be unhelpful for foul prediction. Fouls may be more contact-quality-driven than sequence-driven.

BIP — Weak Positive (BIP Probability)

BIP probability z-scores are real but moderate: lag1-geom z = +9.4, alllag-geom z = +16.6. The model correctly predicts whether a ball is put in play (high geometry tercile: 21.5% BIP rate vs 16.3% low). However, the run-value-weighted SHAP vs real delta_run_exp t-tests are near-null (t ≈ 1.4–1.6, n=5,870 BIP events) — geometry helps predict IF contact happens, not HOW HARD it's hit. Contact quality appears uncorrelated with pitch geometry.

Cross-Classifier Pattern

A clear pattern emerges by pitch-selection vs. contact category:

  • Pitch selection (swing, take): geometry strongly predicts (z ≈ +40–+153)
  • BIP probability: geometry weakly predicts (z ≈ +9–+17)
  • Whiff: geometry null (z ≈ ±1.7)
  • Foul: geometry inverts (z ≈ −22 to −45)

Tunneling geometry appears to capture "does this pitch confuse the hitter into the wrong decision?" — real for swing/take, irrelevant once bat is committed.

The geometry signal is classifier-specific: it is real and meaningful for pitch-selection outcomes (swing/take) but null or inverted for contact-quality outcomes (whiff, foul). Geometry weakly predicts BIP probability but not contact quality. This is consistent with a theory where geometric similarity affects whether hitters swing and miss (decision-making) but not what happens when they make contact.

Why the YoY Collapse Happens

The sequencing features (same_as_lag1, pitch_n_in_pa, n_distinct_prior_types, arsenal_entropy) are all within-game or within-season signals. A pitcher who throws a lot of same-pitch repetitions in 2021 may deliberately vary their arsenal in 2022. These features don't represent stable pitcher characteristics — they represent game-by-game tactical choices. The high within-season split-half (0.946) confirms the model is consistent within a season; it's just not measuring anything that predicts next year's outcomes.

The negative xRV-whiff correlation (r=−0.138) compounds this: the model has learned that high-entropy arsenals (more pitch variety) predict better outcomes by xRV, but variety and whiff rate are apparently inversely related in the real data. The model is measuring something real within a season but pointing in the wrong direction for the outcome that matters most.

What Sequencing+ v1 Tells Us

Pitch-type sequencing without geometry is insufficient as a standalone construct. The features capture tactical variability (how much pitchers mix pitches) but not outcome-relevant quality. The correct conclusion is not "geometry is useless" — it's that geometry contributes the outcome-relevant signal for swing/take decisions that pure sequencing features cannot replicate.

What these diagnostics establish:

  • Geometry is real for swing/take decisions (xSwing%, xCS%: z > 40). The model is legitimately learning that pitch trajectory similarity affects whether hitters swing.
  • Geometry is null for whiff prediction (z ≈ ±1.7). xWhiff% is sequence-driven, not geometry-driven.
  • Geometry inverts for foul prediction (z ≈ −22 to −45). This may warrant removing geometry from the xFoul% classifier in a future v2.1.

Sequencing+ v1 fails the battery: YoY r=0.030 vs Tunneling+ v2's ~0.40–0.55, and xRV correlates negatively with real whiff% (r=−0.138). It is NOT a viable replacement for Tunneling+ v2. The geometry-inversion in xFoul% is a real issue, but the fix is targeted (remove geometry from the xFoul% classifier specifically) rather than wholesale geometry removal.

Implication for v3 architecture: Keep geometry for xSwing%, xCS%, and BIP classifiers where it carries real signal (+41 to +153 z-scores). Remove geometry from xFoul% (z≈−22 to −45, wrong direction). For xWhiff%, geometry is null — but pitch-type sequencing IS real (z=18.6), so the current architecture is fine for whiff even without the geometry features contributing.

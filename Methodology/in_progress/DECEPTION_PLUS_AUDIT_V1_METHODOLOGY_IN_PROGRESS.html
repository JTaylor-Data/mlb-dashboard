Rolling window vs. season-level aggregation

Recommendation: defer. Season-level aggregation is the correct default.
Current implementation: arm_angle_std, release_extension_std, release_consistency are all computed as the season-long standard deviation per (pitcher_id, season) — every pitch that pitcher throws in a season gets the same value. A rolling-window alternative (std over last N pitches) would capture mid-season mechanical adjustments but introduces look-ahead bias unless carefully caretaken. The season-level framing aligns with how Deception+ is positioned (a season-level assessment), and the feature already has strong SHAP contribution as-is. Rolling windows are a future enhancement, not a present need.

Part 6 — Deception+ Under Decomposed-Classifier Architecture

Hypothesis confirmed: mechanics features produce near-coin-flip outcome classifiers. Do not promote.
All 4 binary classifiers returned AUC 0.51–0.53 in LOSO CV — within ~1–2% of random chance. This is expected and documented. The decomposed architecture only helps when features directly drive outcome classification (plate position → called strike, pitch shape → swing). Delivery mechanics are too indirect for that.

The YoY stability paradox. The decomposed architecture achieves dramatically higher YoY stability (0.783 vs 0.472) — but this is not an achievement, it's a symptom. Delivery mechanics (arm slot, release consistency) are effectively time-invariant per pitcher: Tyler Rogers threw submarines in 2021 and 2022. The decomposed model's high stability means it's learning pitcher identity, not per-pitch outcome contribution. The live Huber regressor trades some stability for a much stronger ERA signal (+0.564 vs +0.311), which is the correct tradeoff for a model that's supposed to grade deception quality, not just identify consistent deliveries.
Cross-model correlation with Stuff+. The decomposed version correlates more strongly with Stuff+ (r = 0.524 vs r = 0.396 for live v1). This makes sense: the decomposed architecture's high-stability scores are effectively capturing "pitchers with unusual mechanics" which partially overlaps with "pitchers with unusual pitch shapes" (what Stuff+ captures). Live v1's lower Stuff+ correlation is evidence it's capturing something more genuinely distinct.

Why the classifiers are near-coin-flip

The gap between delivery mechanics and per-pitch outcome classification has a clear causal structure:

  • Location+ v2: plate_x/plate_z directly determine whether an umpire calls a strike. xCS% AUC = 0.981 because the feature IS the outcome (up to the strike zone boundary).
  • Stuff+ features: velo, spin, break directly shape whether a hitter swings and makes contact. xWhiff% AUC = 0.754.
  • Deception+ features: arm slot, release height, delivery consistency are ONE hop further removed. They influence the hitter's visual read, which influences the swing decision, which influences the outcome. The classifiers are working (0.51–0.53 > 0.50), but the 0.01–0.03 lift above chance doesn't justify a five-classifier architecture.

The live Huber regressor bypasses the classification step and directly predicts delta_run_exp, which aggregates the effect of all these indirect mechanisms. That's the right level of abstraction for this feature set.
